# 0102. Traffic simulator: implementation details

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Requirement IDs:** SC-02, SC-03, RL-03, RL-05, RL-08, RL-09, RL-11 – RL-14, RL-21 – RL-26, GW-13, GW-14, UI-01, CP-03, CP-04, CP-20, NFR-15

## Context

ADR-0096 §1 and §2 settle `demo-traffic run --scenario=base|d1…d7` (with `--check`), `demo-traffic probe`, the `demo-traffic` compose service and `make demo-d1` … `make demo-d7`; ADR-0098 §2 settles each scenario's verify-m8 check, with ADR-0101 §13's D6 budget rule. Building them (task traffic-sim, ADR-0093) met one point that cannot be built as written — D4's stricter policy as a consumer-level policy cannot be enforced next to `consumer-plan` — and left details open. Accepted ADRs are not edited, so the choices are recorded here.

## Decision

1. **D4's `orders-guard` is a route-level policy** keyed `{consumer}` (`match: {routes: [orders]}`, GCRA 50/s burst 50, shadow, `fail_open`), not consumer-level as ADR-0096 §2 and ADR-0098 §D4 say. The control plane's conflict check (CP-03 as ADR-0037 narrows it, RL-06) refuses two **enforce** policies at the same level with the same algorithm and key template on the same route: as consumer-level, the shadow publish passed (a shadow policy is no conflict) but the promote failed validation ("conflicts with policy consumer-plan: … make them one policy with several limits (RL-06), or put one in shadow mode"). At route level, the limit, its key (one counter per consumer), the shadow report's keys, the 429s it gives `enterprise-co-web` (64% in the enforce window, as ADR-0098 d4.4 expects) and every D4 rule are unchanged; the policy shows as route level on the Policies screen. This amends ADR-0096 §2's and ADR-0098 §D4's wording ("consumer-level"); the user accepted it on 2026-11-28 ("accept recommended in all"). ADR-0096 and ADR-0098 point here.
2. **Rollbacks need only the revision.** `rollbackConfig` takes the working set's revision ETag from `validateConfig` whether the set validates or not (a rollback replaces the working set); a publish still requires it to validate. D4 rolls back to the version current before 0 s on **every** exit path (a failed step, SIGINT, SIGTERM), so a stricter policy is never left in the demo.
3. **Loads.** `internal/loadgen` (moved from `cmd/loadgen`, no behaviour change to loadgen) gives `Do` each request's due time and gains `Per` (a rate per period), for `free-co-web`'s 0.5 rps on `payments`. Every answer is placed by its due time from time 0, the first scheduled request, which is 200 ms after setup.
4. **Keys.** Each run issues one key per consumer it drives (no `expires_at`), then probes every replica until each answers something other than 401 (at most 10 s) **before** time 0, so no measured answer is a 401 of a key not applied yet. The base service checks every 30 s and replaces a key with under 10 minutes left, switching to the new key only once every replica accepts it. Output names keys by ID (`key #412`); `apiKey` prints as that, its secret as `[redacted]`.
5. **Demo mode first.** `run` asks `GET /api/v1/demo` before anything else and stops with `demo mode is off (GET /api/v1/demo answered 404)` (exit 1, and in `--check` every check `FAIL … setup:`), so a scenario never issues keys or sends load to a dev stack.
6. **D2's demo form** floods from 15 s to 45 s of its 60 s (ADR-0096 left the flood's timing open), so the viewer sees the quiet tenants' p99 with and without the flood; the last lines print both. Its check form is ADR-0098's (10 s warm-up, 20 pairs of 10 s slots, loadgen's slot histograms).
7. **d1.4 by each answer's form.** An answer with `Spillway-Redis-Error` (a failure-mode decision) must carry `consumer-plan` with the degraded per-replica `q` (3 free, 33 pro) and `w=1`; `tenant-ceiling` (`fail_open`, so not evaluated in a failure) is required only on the other answers, which must carry both items with ADR-0098's `q` and `w`. An answer with neither `Spillway-Decision-Time` nor a Redis error (the deny cache) is judged in the Redis form.
8. **D5.** Tc is when `setDemoFault redis-cut on` returns; the To scrapes are taken, then To is when `off` is sent. d5.2's "its share" for a replica is the number of `pro-co-mobile` `payments` 503s to that replica due after the Tc scrape was received and answered before the To scrape was sent (exactly the ones the two scrapes bracket). d5.0 and d5.7's 90% hold per route, not pooled. d5.6 also needs at least one Redis error in the cut.
9. **D6.** Ts and Te are when the switches return; a circuit counts as opened or closed at the **receive** time of the first scrape showing it (the later bound); d6.1 and d6.4 judge every `orders` answer (base consumers' and the D6 stream's). The run ends at Te + 30 s.
10. **D7.** Heavy hitters are read with `since` = time 0 **rounded down to its minute**: windows are whole minutes (ADR-0041), so `since` = time 0 would hide the current minute's window for up to 60 s. K₁ is the run's own new key. In `--check` the flood stops when d7.1 holds or at 20 s, and the ten block rounds run either way, so d7.2 – d7.4 are measured even when d7.1 fails. Block probes go to `orders`, one every 50 ms per replica, each on its own goroutine with a 4 s timeout; the sample runs from just before the `createAccessList` request to the replica's first 403 answer. `make demo-d7 BLOCK=1` blocks once and ends.
11. **D4's timing.** Demo: shadow at 0 s, report at 40 s, promote at 45 s, rollback at 75 s, end at 90 s. Check: ADR-0098's (report at 60 s, promote at once, rollback 21 s after the promote is applied, end 11 s after the rollback is applied). A publish's time to applied runs from just before the publish request to the poll (every 100 ms) that finds every replica in the version's `applied` list.
12. **Output.** Every run prints one viewer line a second (base every 10 s; D2 every 5 s) and a summary per tenant and per consumer and route (answers, allowed, denied, 403/401, 503, other, p50, p99). `--check` prints ADR-0098's `PASS`/`FAIL` lines and `dN: PASS (n checks)` or `dN: FAIL (k of n checks: …)` on stderr, exits 1 on any FAIL, and writes the JSON report (checks, per-stream counts, measured values; no key) to `--out`.
13. **The service and the targets.** `demo-traffic` (profile `demo`, label `spillway.demo`, `read_only`, `nofile` 65536, healthcheck `demo-traffic healthcheck` against `127.0.0.1:8090`, which answers 200 once the load runs) is one of `make demo`'s services; `make demo-reset` stops it before dropping the database (its keys go with it) and starts it after the gateways; `make demo-dN` runs `deploy/demo/scenario.sh dN`, which stops it, runs the one-off with `--no-deps` and starts it again on every exit (trap). The fault targets stay on the `demo-seed` one-off (ADR-0101 §7): moving them would change nothing they do.
14. **d6.1's "before the fault" window (amends ADR-0098 d6.1; the user's decision of 2026-11-28, "accept recommended in all", option A).** ADR-0098 said: "**d6.1** Before the fault: every `orders` answer due in [0, Ts) is 2xx, and every replica's `gw_circuit_state{upstream="orders"}` is 0." It now reads: "**d6.1** Before the fault: every `orders` answer that **completed before the `upstream-slow` switch-on request was sent** is 2xx, and every replica's `gw_circuit_state{upstream="orders"}` is 0." The answers due in [0, Ts) that had not completed when the request was sent (in flight then, or due between the send and Ts) are left out of d6.1, and `--check` prints how many and their statuses on d6.1's line; the circuit part, Ts, Te and d6.2 – d6.4 (d6.3 as ADR-0101 §13 gives it) are unchanged. Why: the load runs on through the switch, so a request due a few milliseconds before Ts could still be in flight when toxiproxy added the 4 s latency toxic; its response then came after the route's 2.5 s per-try timeout and it answered 504. One check round in four failed d6.1 that way (3 × 504 among 2,395 answers, every other D6 rule passing; the gateway behaved correctly). The other options were keeping the rule (D6 would then fail some runs by timing alone) or pausing the D6 stream around the switch (it changes the load shape). `test/demo` `TestFaultUpstreamSlow` needs no change: its before-the-fault phase ends, every answer received, before it switches the fault on.

## Consequences

- Every scenario runs from make, prints what the viewer should see, and judges itself by ADR-0098's rules with `--check`, so the verify task wraps the checks without re-implementing them.
- D4 shows a route-level policy; the README's D4 scene and `docs/demo.md` describe it as "a stricter per-consumer limit on orders".
- `make test-demo` grows by about 75 s (`TestScenarioD1`); the base load now runs during the demo's other tests too.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep `orders-guard` consumer-level with another key template** (for example `{consumer}:orders`) — passes the conflict check by a trick, and the shadow report's keys would be digests of a template no other policy uses.
- **A sliding-window `orders-guard`** — passes the check too, but D4 would then compare two algorithms, which the scenario does not mean to show.
- **Change the conflict check** — CP-03's rule is right for real configurations; the demo adapts to it.
